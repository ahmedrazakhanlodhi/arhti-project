## Slide 1. Title
**Where the Bank Ends and the Arhti Begins: Mapping Access, Allocation, and the Cost of Agricultural Credit in Punjab**
Ahmed Raza Khan Lodhi and Hasnain Akram, PPHA 38520, University of Chicago. November 30, 2026.
Central question: does the geography of agricultural finance correspond to where farming happens and who farms?

## Slide 2. Data and methods
- Mouza Census 2020: 23,130 rural populated mouzas, 137 tehsils (availability reported by village respondents).
- SBP FY2023-24 lending by district as reported and holding class; Agricultural Census 2024 farms, area, crops; SBP branch list (30 Jun 2024).
- Moran's I and LISA (Queen, 999 permutations, seed 20261031; FDR cutoff 0.0117); Mantel–Haenszel within tehsils.
- Location quotients; holding-class shares; SBP Monthly Statistical Bulletin rate table (Table 3.33). Sources differ in date and unit.

## Slide 3. Access
- **Headline:** Arhti credit is clustered and often reported alongside bank credit, not mainly in its place.
- **Key number:** odds ratio 1.18 (95% CI 1.09–1.27) within tehsils; Breslow–Day p < 0.001; 67 tehsils above independence, 53 below.
- 31.5% of rural populated mouzas report arhti credit; Moran's I 0.536. Exploratory: higher where more land is cropland (ρ = 0.49).
- **Figures:** 2, 3, 4.

## Slide 4. Allocation
- **Headline:** Formal credit reaches small farms by count, not by amount.
- **Key number:** subsistence holdings are 94.1% of farms and 91.9% of borrower records but 35.9% of disbursement; above-economic holdings are 0.5% of farms and 49.4% of disbursement.
- Small-farm reach ranges from 21.6 to 299.0 per 1,000 farms under 12.5 acres; concentration coefficient 0.297. Larger holdings need larger loans.
- **Figures:** A1, 5, S6.

## Slide 5. Reporting geography
- **Headline:** Lending is recorded heavily in a few city districts.
- **Key number:** five city districts record 39.7% of disbursement and 59.6% of above-economic disbursement, with 13.3% of cultivated area.
- Lahore: 190.4 branches per 10,000 farms in total, 3.9 rural. District = as reported by SBP (attribution under verification).
- **Figures:** R1, R2.

## Slide 6. Cost (context)
- **Headline:** Published arhti cost estimates sit far above formal agricultural rates.
- **Key number:** IGC 2013 implicit arhti cost by crop 61.9%–80.7% vs SBP FY2012-13 averages of 12.00–19.00 percent; FY2023-24 averages 20.67–29.42 percent.
- Kissan Card: 0% for holdings up to 12.5 acres, operational from 15 Oct 2024 (after the data period). Not matched contracts.
- **Figure:** P1.

## Slide 7. District scorecard
- **Headline:** Arhti availability and small-farm reach vary independently across districts.
- **Key number:** 10 high/high, 10 low/low, 8 low arhti + high reach, 7 high arhti + low reach, 1 not classified.
- Descriptive groupings at the median; not rankings.

## Slide 8. Limitations
- Availability reports, not household borrowing; borrower records may not be unique people.
- Different dates and units; SBP district attribution under verification; 36 pre-split districts.
- SBP "up to 12.5 acres" vs census "under 12.5 acres"; sample-based census denominators.
- Published cost estimates come from small, non-representative studies.

## Slide 9. Conclusion
- Agricultural finance in Punjab does not follow farming evenly: arhti credit sits next to formal credit, formal credit concentrates by amount, and lending is recorded in city districts.
- Next: confirm SBP's district attribution; observe how farmers combine arhti, bank and Kissan Card credit.
