## Rural credit: theory and Pakistan

- **Aleem, I. (1990).** Imperfect information, screening, and the costs of informal lending: A study of a rural credit market in
  Pakistan. *The World Bank Economic Review, 4*(3), 329–349. https://doi.org/10.1093/wber/4.3.329
  Use: informal lenders around Chambar (Sindh) tie credit to crop marketing; average annual cost of informal commercial
  borrowing 78.7 percent (range about 18 to 200 percent), against a 12 percent bank rate.
- **Bell, C. (1988).** Credit markets and interlinked transactions. In H. Chenery & T. N. Srinivasan (Eds.), *Handbook of
  Development Economics* (Vol. 1, pp. 763–830). Elsevier. https://doi.org/10.1016/S1573-4471(88)01019-8
  Use: survey of interlinked credit and output contracts (the arrangement the arhti represents).
- **Bhaduri, A. (1977).** On the formation of usurious interest rates in backward agriculture. *Cambridge Journal of Economics,
  1*(4), 341–352. https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordjournals.cje.a035369
  Use: the classic view of high rural interest rates as a product of lender power (cited only for that general position).
- **Haq, A., Aslam, A., Chaudhry, A. A., Naseer, A., Muhammad, K., Mushtaq, K., & Farooqi, M. S. (2013).** *Who is the "arthi":
  Understanding the commission agent's role in the agriculture supply chain* (Working paper). International Growth Centre.
  https://www.theigc.org/sites/default/files/2014/09/Haq-Et-Al-2013-Working-Paper.pdf
  Use: in Punjab the arthi is the largest source of informal agricultural credit, operating from the province's 325 commodity
  markets; implicit annualized interest 62 to 80 percent by crop (Table 11); purposive sample (`docs/literature_evidence.csv`).
- **Hoff, K., & Stiglitz, J. E. (1990).** Introduction: Imperfect information and rural credit markets—Puzzles and policy
  perspectives. *The World Bank Economic Review, 4*(3), 235–250. https://doi.org/10.1093/wber/4.3.235
  Use: screening, incentive and enforcement problems as reasons formal and informal lenders coexist.
- **Khandker, S. R., & Faruqee, R. R. (2003).** The impact of farm credit in Pakistan. *Agricultural Economics, 28*(3), 197–213.
  https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1574-0862.2003.tb00138.x
  Use: large landowners (about 4 percent of rural households) received 42 percent of formal loans; landless and subsistence
  households (more than 69 percent) received 23 percent.
- **Malik, S. J., Mushtaq, M., & Gill, M. A. (1989).** Differential access and the rural credit market in Pakistan: Some recent
  evidence. *The Pakistan Development Review, 28*(4, Part II), 709–716. https://doi.org/10.30541/v28i4IIpp.709-716
  Use: small farm households have limited access to institutional credit.
- **PMN/MEDA (2019).** Warehouse-receipt financing study (survey of 400 farmers in 10 Punjab districts and 4 focus groups, 2018);
  as recorded in `docs/literature_evidence.csv` (verified_by_ahmed pending).
- **Qureshi, S., Nabi, I., & Faruqee, R. (1996).** *Rural finance for growth and poverty alleviation* (Policy Research Working
  Paper No. 1593). World Bank.
  https://documents.worldbank.org/en/publication/documents-reports/documentdetail/457081468775577395
  Use: fewer than a third of rural households borrow, and only about 10 percent of their loans come from institutional sources.
- **Smith, L. E. D., Stockbridge, M., & Lohano, H. R. (1999).** Facilitating the provision of farm credit: The role of
  interlocking transactions between traders and zamindars in crop marketing systems in Sindh. *World Development, 27*(2),
  403–418. https://doi.org/10.1016/S0305-750X(98)00140-5
  Use: trader credit interlocked with input supply and crop purchase behaved more competitively than exploitatively.

## Geography of formal finance

- **Burgess, R., & Pande, R. (2005).** Do rural banks matter? Evidence from the Indian social banking experiment. *American
  Economic Review, 95*(3), 780–795. https://doi.org/10.1257/0002828054201242
  Use: where rural bank branches are located matters for rural outcomes (India, 1977–1990).
- **Leyshon, A., French, S., & Signoretta, P. (2008).** Financial exclusion and the geography of bank and building society branch
  closure in Britain. *Transactions of the Institute of British Geographers, 33*(4), 447–465.
  https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1475-5661.2008.00323.x
  Use: precedent for mapping physical access to finance; closures concentrated in poorer areas.
- **Mian, A. (2006).** Distance constraints: The limits of foreign lending in poor economies. *The Journal of Finance, 61*(3),
  1465–1505. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2006.00878.x
  Use: distance shapes bank lending in Pakistan (80,000 corporate loans; head-office to branch distance).
